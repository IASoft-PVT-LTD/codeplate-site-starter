A check-in is only useful if your teammates read it. These are the habits we see in the updates that get read.

## 1. Lead with the change

Start with what is different since yesterday, not with what you are "continuing to work on".

> **Instead of:** Continued working on the export feature.
>
> **Try:** CSV export now handles dates; Excel export is next.

## 2. Name the blocker and the person

"Waiting on design" is easy to scroll past. "Need Tomás to confirm the empty state by Thursday" gets an answer.

## 3. Link, don't explain

If the details live in an issue or a pull request, link it. One line of context is enough.

## 4. Keep it under a minute to read

If an update needs more than five lines, it probably wants to be a document of its own.

## 5. Say what went well

A short "shipped the new onboarding, signups look good" makes the digest a nicer place to be. Teams that share wins keep checking in.
